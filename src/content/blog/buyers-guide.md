Choosing claims software is an operational decision, not simply a technology purchase. The right platform should help a driver submit better evidence, help a shop produce a clear estimate, and help an adjuster make a confident decision—all while giving fleet leaders a reliable view of downtime and cost.

## Start with the workflow

Map a claim from first notice of loss through repair completion. Document every handoff, duplicate entry, approval, and report. Ask vendors to demonstrate that exact workflow with realistic photos and edge cases, rather than relying on a slide deck.

## Evaluate AI capabilities carefully

Look for guided photo capture, damage detection, confidence indicators, and explainable recommendations. AI should identify what it sees and what it cannot determine. Confirm that a qualified person can edit, approve, and audit every AI-assisted estimate. Ask how the model handles poor lighting, multiple damaged areas, and unfamiliar vehicle configurations.

## Check estimate quality and control

A useful estimate workflow supports labor and parts line items, supplements, notes, approvals, and version history. It should make assumptions visible and allow your team to apply its own policies. Request sample outputs and understand how pricing data is sourced and refreshed.

## Prioritize integrations

Your claims system must fit the tools you already use. Ask about connections to CCC ONE, Mitchell, Audatex, and accounting platforms such as QuickBooks. Clarify whether integrations are native, partner-supported, or dependent on custom exports. A documented REST API, webhooks, and stable identifiers are important if you plan to connect fleet, shop, or insurer systems later.

## Roles, mobile access, and adoption

Fleet managers, drivers, technicians, adjusters, and administrators need different permissions. Require role-based access, assignment controls, and an audit trail. Mobile access should support fast uploads and status checks in the field, even when the experience is used on a phone rather than a desktop.

## Analytics that drive action

Dashboards should expose cycle time, open claims, downtime, supplement rates, repair spend, and bottlenecks. A polished chart is less important than exportable, trustworthy data that can answer: where are claims waiting, and what should we fix next?

## Security and implementation

Ask about encryption, retention, tenant isolation, backups, and incident response. Review the data processing terms before sharing sensitive images or claim details. Finally, define an onboarding plan with an owner, pilot group, success metrics, training, and a migration strategy.

The best choice is the platform your teams will use consistently. Favor transparent AI, flexible integrations, clear ownership, and measurable outcomes over a long feature list.
